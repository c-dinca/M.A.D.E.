## Context

What forced a decision. The constraint, the failure it prevents, or the requirement it serves. Two to
four paragraphs. Do not describe the solution here.

## Decision

What is now true, stated so that a reader can comply without inference. Use MUST and MUST NOT where the
decision is a rule that code or review will enforce.

## Alternatives considered

### <Alternative name> — rejected

State the case **an advocate for this option would make**, at its strongest. Then the reason it lost. If
the reason is operational cost, team size, or timing rather than technical merit, say so plainly; that
is usually the honest answer and it is the one that makes the trigger below meaningful.

### <Second alternative> — rejected

As above. At least one real alternative is mandatory. If there was genuinely no alternative, this was
not a decision and does not need an ADR.

## Consequences

### Positive

What this buys, concretely.

### Negative — mandatory

What this costs. What becomes harder, slower, or impossible. What a future maintainer will curse. If
this section is empty the ADR is not finished.

## Revisit when

A measurement, a scale threshold, or a customer requirement that reopens the question. Not "when it
becomes a problem".
